# Michael Jordan

Michael Jeffrey Jordan is a former shooting guard and small forward, primarily for the Chicago Bulls. He was born on February 17, 1963, in Brooklyn, New York, and grew up in Wilmington, North Carolina. Listed at 6-foot-6, he is nicknamed Air Jordan and His Airness. Many consider him the greatest basketball player of all time. The Chicago Bulls drafted him third overall in 1984 after Hakeem Olajuwon and Sam Bowie.

## Early life and college

Jordan was cut from Laney High School's varsity as a 5-foot-11 sophomore, then grew four inches and averaged more than 25 points as a junior and senior. He took jersey 23. North Carolina recruited him to play for Dean Smith. As a freshman he hit the game-winning jumper in the 1982 NCAA title game against Georgetown and Patrick Ewing. He was national college player of the year as a junior in 1984, then left after three seasons. He later finished a geography degree in 1986. North Carolina retired his No. 23.

## Chicago Bulls (1984–1993, 1995–1998)

Jordan won Rookie of the Year in 1985 at 28.2 points per game. A broken foot limited him to 18 games in 1985–86; in the playoffs he scored 63 at Boston Garden, still a playoff record for a single game. In 1986–87 he averaged 37.1 points, the only non-Wilt Chamberlain season of 3,000 points. In 1988 he won MVP and Defensive Player of the Year in the same season, a feat later matched by Hakeem Olajuwon and Giannis Antetokounmpo.

Detroit's Bad Boys and the "Jordan Rules" stopped Chicago in the East from 1988 to 1990. The Shot over Craig Ehlo beat Cleveland in 1989. Jordan scored a career-high 69 against the Cavaliers in 1990.

With Scottie Pippen and coach Phil Jackson's triangle offense, Chicago three-peated: 1991 over the Lakers (the mid-air switch dunk in Game 2), 1992 over Portland (35 first-half points in Game 1), and 1993 over Phoenix at a Finals-record 41.0 points per game. Jordan was Finals MVP each year.

He retired in October 1993 after his father was murdered, citing exhaustion. He played outfield for the White Sox's Double-A Birmingham Barons (.202, 3 HR, 51 RBI, 30 steals) and the Arizona Fall League. On March 18, 1995, he announced "I'm back," first wearing 45, then 23. Orlando beat Chicago that spring.

In 1995–96 the Bulls went 72–10, then a record, with Dennis Rodman. They beat Seattle for title four. In 1997 came The Flu Game against Utah (38 points while ill). In 1998 Game 6 he stole from Karl Malone and hit the jumper over Bryon Russell for a second three-peat. Six titles, six Finals MVPs, never a Game 7 in the Finals.

## Washington Wizards (2001–2003)

Jordan retired again in 1999, then became Wizards president of basketball operations. He returned as a player in 2001, donating his salary after 9/11. He averaged 22.9 then 20.0 points at ages 38–40. Washington missed the playoffs both years. His last game was April 16, 2003, in Philadelphia. The Bulls and Miami Heat retired No. 23.

## Championships and honors

Jordan has six NBA titles (1991–1993, 1996–1998), six Finals MVPs (a record), five regular-season MVPs (1988, 1991, 1992, 1996, 1998), Defensive Player of the Year (1988), Rookie of the Year (1985), 10 scoring titles (a record), three steals titles, 14 All-Star selections, and two Slam Dunk Contest wins. He holds NBA records for career regular-season scoring average (30.1) and playoff scoring average (33.4). He is in the Naismith Hall of Fame (2009) and the NBA 50th and 75th Anniversary Teams. The league MVP trophy is named for him. President Obama awarded him the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016.

## Playing style

Jordan combined scoring, defense, and competitiveness. Early on he dunked from the free-throw line; later he posted up with a fadeaway. He averaged 5.3 assists and 6.2 rebounds for a guard, with 2.3 steals. He decided 25 Bulls games in the last 30 seconds. Critics once called him selfish; he deferred more in the triangle. Three-point shooting was not a strength (.327 career).

## Olympics, baseball, and business

He won gold at the 1984 Olympics and with the 1992 Dream Team, plus the 1983 Pan American Games and 1992 Tournament of the Americas — unbeaten in 30 national-team games. He starred in Space Jam (1996) and was the focus of The Last Dance (2020). Nike's Air Jordan line, launched in 1984, remains a global brand. He was majority owner of the Charlotte Hornets until 2023 and co-owns NASCAR's 23XI Racing. As of 2026 his net worth is reported around $4.3 billion.

## Career statistics

Jordan's NBA career line is 1,072 games, 38.3 minutes, 30.1 points (a record average), 6.2 rebounds, 5.3 assists, and 2.3 steals, on .497 / .327 / .835 shooting. Playoffs: 33.4 points (a record average) in 179 games. College at North Carolina: 17.7 points in 101 games.

Season highs include 37.1 points (1986–87), 8.0 assists and 8.0 rebounds (1988–89), and 3.2 steals (1987–88). Playoff high: 43.7 points per game in 1986.
