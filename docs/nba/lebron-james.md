# LeBron James

LeBron Raymone James Sr. is a small forward and power forward for the Philadelphia 76ers of the National Basketball Association. He was born on December 30, 1984, in Akron, Ohio, and is listed at 6-foot-9 and 250 pounds. Nicknamed King James, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest basketball players ever. He entered the NBA from St. Vincent–St. Mary High School as the first overall pick in 2003.

## Early life and high school

James was born at Summa Health Akron Campus to Gloria James. He grew up in Akron and began organized basketball in fifth grade. With friends Sian Cotton, Dru Joyce III, and Willie McGee — a group they called the Fab Four — he played AAU ball for the Northeast Ohio Shooting Stars and later enrolled at St. Vincent–St. Mary, a private Catholic school.

As a freshman he averaged 18 points and 6 rebounds and helped the Fighting Irish go 27–0 and win an Ohio Division III state title. As a sophomore he averaged 25.2 points, 7.2 rebounds, 5.8 assists, and 3.8 steals; some home games moved to the University of Akron's Rhodes Arena because of demand from fans and scouts. He was named Ohio Mr. Basketball and made the USA Today All-USA First Team, the first sophomore to do either.

As a junior he appeared on the cover of Sports Illustrated, the first high school basketball underclassman to do so, averaged 29 points, and became the first junior named Gatorade National Boys Basketball Player of the Year. As a senior he averaged 31.6 points, 9.6 rebounds, 4.6 assists, and 3.4 steals, won Ohio Mr. Basketball and USA Today All-USA First Team for a third straight year, and was Gatorade National Player of the Year again. St. Vincent–St. Mary won three state titles in four years. James also played wide receiver in high school football and was named first-team all-state; Notre Dame and other Division I programs recruited him before a wrist injury ended his senior football season.

He skipped college as a prep-to-pro. Cleveland selected him first overall in the 2003 NBA draft. He chose jersey number 23 in honor of Michael Jordan.

## Cleveland Cavaliers (2003–2010)

In his NBA debut, James scored 25 points in a loss to the Sacramento Kings, then a record for a prep-to-pro player in a first game. He won Rookie of the Year in 2004 and was only the third player to average at least 20 points, 5 rebounds, and 5 assists as a rookie.

He made his first All-Star Game in 2004–05 and scored 56 points against Toronto that season, then a Cavaliers single-game record. Cleveland reached the playoffs in 2006 for the first time since 1998. In 2007 he led the Cavaliers to their first NBA Finals; in Eastern Conference finals Game 5 against Detroit he scored 48 points, including Cleveland's last 25, in a double-overtime win. San Antonio swept the Finals.

In 2007–08 he won the scoring title at 30.0 points per game and passed Brad Daugherty as Cleveland's all-time leading scorer. He won regular-season MVP in 2009 and 2010. The 2008–09 Cavaliers went 66–16, a franchise record at the time. Cleveland lost in the 2009 Eastern Conference finals to Orlando and in the 2010 second round to Boston.

## The Decision and Miami Heat (2010–2014)

James became an unrestricted free agent in July 2010. On an ESPN special titled The Decision, broadcast from a Boys & Girls Club in Greenwich, Connecticut, he said he would take his talents to South Beach and join the Miami Heat with Dwyane Wade and Chris Bosh. The show raised millions for charity but drew heavy criticism in Cleveland. Owner Dan Gilbert published an open letter to fans; some supporters burned James jerseys. James later said he regretted how the announcement was handled. In Miami he switched from 23 to 6 because Miami had retired 23 for Jordan.

The Heat lost the 2011 Finals to Dallas. James's scoring dropped sharply in that series. After working with Hakeem Olajuwon on his post game, he won MVP and his first title in 2012 against Oklahoma City, then repeated in 2013 against San Antonio. He was Finals MVP both years. In 2012–13 Miami won 27 straight games, third-longest in NBA history, and James won his fourth MVP, one vote short of unanimous. In 2013–14 he scored a career-high 61 points against Charlotte. The Heat returned to the Finals and lost to the Spurs in five games.

## Return to Cleveland (2014–2018)

James opted out of Miami in 2014 and returned to Cleveland on a two-year deal, switching back to number 23. The announcement was received far more warmly than The Decision.

In 2015 Cleveland reached the Finals and lost to Golden State. James became the first player since the 1960s to play in five straight Finals. In 2016 the Cavaliers finished with the East's best record and met the Warriors again, who had won a record 73 regular-season games. Cleveland fell behind 3–1. James scored 41 in Games 5 and 6, then posted a Game 7 triple-double and blocked Andre Iguodala in a play remembered as "The Block." Cleveland became the first team to win a Finals after trailing 3–1. It was the city's first major professional sports title in 52 years. James was unanimous Finals MVP.

In 2017 he passed Michael Jordan as the NBA's all-time postseason scoring leader and averaged a Finals triple-double (33.6 points, 12.0 rebounds, 10.0 assists) in a five-game loss to Golden State. In 2018 he scored a playoff career-high 51 points in Finals Game 1 before Cleveland was swept. That made eight consecutive Finals appearances from 2011 through 2018.

## Los Angeles Lakers (2018–2026)

James signed with the Lakers in 2018 on a four-year contract. A groin injury in 2018–19 cost him 17 games and ended his playoff streak; it was his first missed postseason since 2005.

After the Lakers acquired Anthony Davis, James played full-time point guard in 2019–20 and led the league in assists (10.2 per game) for the first time. In the bubble Finals he beat Miami and won his fourth title and fourth Finals MVP, the first player to take that award with three franchises.

Later Lakers seasons mixed records with injuries. In 2022 he became the only player with at least 10,000 career points, 10,000 rebounds, and 10,000 assists. On February 7, 2023, he passed Kareem Abdul-Jabbar as the NBA's all-time leading scorer; the league stopped the game for an on-court ceremony. In December 2023 he won the inaugural NBA In-Season Tournament (NBA Cup) and was named tournament MVP. In 2024 he became the first player to 40,000 regular-season points.

On October 22, 2024, James and his son Bronny — drafted 55th by the Lakers — became the first father-son duo to play in an NBA game together. James later became the oldest player to score 40 in a game and the oldest to record a triple-double. In 2025–26 he played a 23rd season, an NBA record, then passed Abdul-Jabbar for most career field goals, Robert Parish for most games, and Abdul-Jabbar for most career wins. He also reached 12,000 assists and 300 playoff games. On June 30, 2026, he told the Lakers he would not return.

## Philadelphia 76ers (2026–present)

On July 24, 2026, James announced on X that he would sign with the Philadelphia 76ers, calling it his "last decision." Pennsylvania's governor declared that date LeBron James Day in the state. He signed a two-year, $8 million contract with a player option on July 26. Philadelphia is his fourth NBA team.

## Championships, honors, and records

James has four NBA titles: Miami in 2012 and 2013, Cleveland in 2016, and the Lakers in 2020. He has ten Finals appearances. Individual honors include four regular-season MVPs (2009, 2010, 2012, 2013), four Finals MVPs, Rookie of the Year (2004), three All-Star Game MVPs (2006, 2008, 2018), the 2008 scoring title, the 2020 assists title, 2023 NBA Cup MVP, and 2024 Olympics MVP. He is a record 22-time All-Star and a 21-time All-NBA selection, including a record 13 First Team nods, plus six All-Defensive teams.

League records include most seasons (23), most games, most minutes, most field goals, and all-time scoring. He ranks among the career leaders in assists and steals. He was named to the NBA 75th Anniversary Team and has been AP Male Athlete of the Decade for the 2010s.

## Playing style

James has spent most of his career at small forward and power forward but has also run point. Early on he was an on-ball point forward: a slasher and finisher whose size and speed created mismatches, while critics questioned his jumper and post game. In Miami, Erik Spoelstra used him more in the post and as an off-ball cutter, and his efficiency rose. His playmaking — full-court vision, passes after leaving his feet, and gravity that forces help — is often ranked with the best passers in NBA history.

Defense improved from a weak point as a rookie to All-Defensive honors from 2009 to 2014. In Miami he guarded all five positions. Later in his career analysts noted less regular-season defensive effort, which James himself called "chill mode," with a reputation for raising intensity in the playoffs ("Playoff LeBron").

## Olympics and Team USA

James debuted for the United States at the 2004 Athens Olympics as a 19-year-old and won bronze, mostly from the bench. He added bronze at the 2006 FIBA World Championship. Team USA went undefeated for gold in Beijing 2008. In London 2012 he recorded the first triple-double in U.S. Olympic men's basketball history against Australia, scored 19 in the gold-medal game against Spain, and became Team USA's all-time leading scorer. That year he joined Michael Jordan as a player who won NBA MVP, an NBA title, Finals MVP, and Olympic gold in the same year.

After a 12-year Olympic hiatus he captained the 2024 Paris team at age 39, was one of two U.S. flag bearers at the opening ceremony (the first male basketball player so honored), led the team in rebounds and assists, and became the first player with two Olympic triple-doubles. The U.S. beat France for gold. James averaged 14.2 points, 6.8 rebounds, and 8.5 assists and was named FIBA Men's Olympics MVP. He is one of three men with three Olympic basketball golds.

## Off the court

James married Savannah Brinson in 2013. They have two sons, Bronny and Bryce, and a daughter, Zhuri. He is the first active NBA player reported to have accumulated $1 billion in career earnings.

The LeBron James Family Foundation is based in Akron. In 2018 it opened I Promise School, a public elementary school for struggling students, which James has called his most important professional accomplishment. Related projects include scholarships with the University of Akron, I Promise Village housing, House Three-Thirty, and I Promise Health Quarters. He has been first vice president of the National Basketball Players Association.

His production company SpringHill (with Maverick Carter) has made documentaries and series including More Than a Game, about his high school years. He is represented by Klutch Sports Group, founded by longtime friend Rich Paul.

## Career statistics

Through 2025–26, James's regular-season career line is 1,622 games (an NBA record), 1,620 starts, 37.6 minutes, 26.8 points, 7.5 rebounds, 7.4 assists, 1.5 steals, and 0.7 blocks per game, on .507 field-goal, .348 three-point, and .737 free-throw shooting. In the playoffs he has 302 games (also a record) and averages 28.2 points, 8.9 rebounds, 7.2 assists, 1.7 steals, and 1.0 blocks in 41.2 minutes, on .495 / .332 / .741 shooting. All-Star Game averages: 21.4 points, 5.8 rebounds, and 5.6 assists in 21 games.

Season highs include 31.4 points (2005–06), 10.2 assists and a league assists title (2019–20), 42.5 minutes (2005–06), and .567 field-goal shooting (2013–14). He led the league in scoring in 2007–08 at 30.0 points per game. Playoff scoring peaks include 35.3 points per game in 2009 and 34.0 in 2018.

High school (St. Vincent–St. Mary): 18.0 points as a freshman; 25.2 points, 7.2 rebounds, 5.8 assists, 3.8 steals as a sophomore; 29.0 points, 8.3 rebounds, 5.7 assists, 3.3 steals as a junior; 31.6 points, 9.6 rebounds, 4.6 assists, 3.4 steals as a senior.

Olympics (Paris 2024): 14.2 points, 6.8 rebounds, 8.5 assists.
