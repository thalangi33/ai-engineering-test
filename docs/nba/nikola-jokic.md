# Nikola Jokić

Nikola Jokić is a center for the Denver Nuggets. He was born on February 19, 1995, in Sombor, Serbia, and is listed at 6-foot-11 and 284 pounds. Nickname: the Joker. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time and as the greatest passing big man in NBA history. Denver drafted him in the second round, 41st overall, in 2014.

## Early life and Mega Basket

Jokić grew up in a two-bedroom apartment in Sombor with his parents, grandmother, and two older brothers, Strahinja and Nemanja. His father was an agricultural engineer. He also competed in amateur harness racing, a hobby he still keeps. He played youth ball for Vojvodina Srbijagas, then signed with Mega Vizura (later Mega Basket) in December 2012.

In 2013–14 he averaged 11.4 points, 6.4 rebounds, and 2.5 assists in the Adriatic League. Denver selected him 41st in 2014; on the ESPN telecast the pick ran on a ticker under a Taco Bell commercial. He stayed in Europe one more year. In 2014–15 he averaged 15.4 points, a league-leading 9.3 rebounds, and 3.5 assists, won ABA League MVP and Top Prospect, then left for the NBA.

## Denver Nuggets (2015–present)

Jokić signed with Denver in July 2015. As a rookie he made All-Rookie First Team and finished third in Rookie of the Year voting. In 2016–17 he moved into the starting lineup in mid-December — a date Nuggets fans later called Jokmas — and posted six triple-doubles, finishing second in Most Improved Player voting.

On February 15, 2018, he recorded the fastest triple-double in NBA history (14 minutes, 33 seconds) with 30 points, 15 rebounds, and 17 assists against Milwaukee. Denver missed the playoffs on the final day. In 2018–19 he signed a five-year max, made his first All-Star team, and earned All-NBA First Team. In the playoffs he had a triple-double in his debut, scored 43 against San Antonio, then logged 65 minutes in a quadruple-overtime loss to Portland.

In the 2020 bubble Denver became the first team to erase two 3–1 deficits in one postseason. Jokić had a 16-point, 22-rebound, 13-assist Game 7 against the Clippers. The Lakers beat Denver in the Western Conference finals.

He won MVP in 2020–21, the first Nugget and the lowest-drafted player ever to win it, and the first center since Shaquille O'Neal in 2000. He won it again in 2021–22 while posting a then-record player efficiency rating of 32.85 and becoming the first player to average 25-plus points, 13-plus rebounds, and seven-plus assists.

In 2022–23 he finished second in MVP voting, signed a $264 million supermax, and led Denver to its first NBA title. He won Western Conference finals MVP against the Lakers, then unanimous Finals MVP against Miami — again the lowest draft pick to win that award. He led the entire postseason in points, rebounds, and assists, a first. In Game 3 of the Finals he had 32 points, 21 rebounds, and 10 assists, the first 30/20/10 Finals game.

He won a third MVP in 2023–24. In 2024–25 he averaged a triple-double (29.6 points, 12.7 rebounds, 10.2 assists), recorded a 61-point triple-double (the highest-scoring one in NBA history), and became the first player with a 30/20/20 game (31 points, 21 rebounds, 22 assists). In 2025–26 he averaged a triple-double again (27.7 / 12.9 / 10.7) and became the first player to lead the league in both rebounds and assists in the same season. He passed Kareem Abdul-Jabbar as the all-time assists leader among centers and moved into second place all-time in triple-doubles.

Because he was a second-round pick, Jokić is often cited as the biggest draft steal of his era, and as the lowest drafted player to win either MVP or Finals MVP.

## Championships and awards

He led Denver to the 2023 NBA championship, the franchise's first title, and won Finals MVP that year. He has three regular-season MVP awards (2021, 2022, and 2024) and six straight top-two MVP finishes, tying Bill Russell and Larry Bird. He is an eight-time All-Star and eight-time All-NBA selection, including six First Team nods. He won Western Conference finals MVP in 2023, All-Rookie First Team in 2016, and Serbian Player of the Year multiple times.

## Playing style

Jokić is known as a playmaking center: full-court outlets, no-look interior passes, and post vision more typical of a point guard. He often runs the offense from the elbow or the dunker spot, pausing to read help before throwing one-handed, water-polo-style passes. Teammates say they have to stay ready in practice or the ball hits them in the face.

He is also a versatile scorer. The Sombor Shuffle — a high-arcing fadeaway off the right foot, named for his hometown — came from rehabbing an ankle injury in 2017 and draws comparisons to Dirk Nowitzki's one-legged fadeaway. He is a career double-double threat in points and rebounds, with assists that rival many starting guards. Despite a famously poor vertical jump, he has posted elite defensive box plus-minus, relying on positioning and IQ more than athleticism.

## Olympics and Serbia

Jokić won silver with Serbia's U-19 team in 2013. With the senior team he won Olympic silver in Rio 2016 (losing the final to the United States) and bronze in Paris 2024. In Paris he averaged 18.8 points, 10.7 rebounds, and 8.7 assists, led the tournament in points, rebounds, assists, and steals, posted a triple-double in the bronze-medal game, and made the Olympics All-Star Five. At EuroBasket 2025 he scored a Serbian tournament-record 39 against Latvia.

## Off the court

Jokić keeps a low public profile relative to his stardom and still spends offseasons around Sombor and harness racing. His brothers remain close; Nemanja played college basketball in the United States.

## Career statistics

Through 2025–26, Jokić's regular-season career line is 810 games, 32.0 minutes, 22.2 points, 11.1 rebounds, 7.5 assists, 1.3 steals, and 0.7 blocks, on .561 / .362 / .825 shooting. Playoff averages: 27.4 points, 12.4 rebounds, and 7.7 assists in 100 games. He is second all-time in career triple-doubles. ABA League with Mega Basket: 11.4 then 15.4 points per game in his two full seasons.

Season highs include 29.6 points with a triple-double average (2024–25), 12.9 rebounds and 10.7 assists as league leader (2025–26), and a 61-point triple-double. Playoff highs include 31.0 points per game in 2022 and 53 points in the 2023 conference semifinals.
