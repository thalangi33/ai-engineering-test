# Stephen Curry

Wardell Stephen Curry II is a point guard for the Golden State Warriors. He was born on March 14, 1988, in Akron, Ohio, and is listed at 6-foot-2 and 185 pounds. Nicknamed Chef Curry and the Baby-Faced Assassin, he is widely regarded as the greatest shooter in basketball history. He played college basketball at Davidson before the Warriors took him seventh overall in 2009.

## Early life and college

Curry grew up in Charlotte, North Carolina, where his father Dell spent most of his NBA career with the Hornets. His mother, Sonya, is an educator. The family briefly lived in Toronto while Dell finished with the Raptors; Steph led Queensway Christian College to an undefeated season and won a provincial title with club team Toronto 5–0. Back in Charlotte he starred at Charlotte Christian School. Virginia Tech, Dell's alma mater, offered only a walk-on spot. Davidson recruited him from tenth grade.

As a freshman he averaged 21.5 points, set Davidson's freshman scoring record, and broke the NCAA freshman three-point record. As a sophomore he averaged 25.9 points and led Davidson to the 2008 Elite Eight, scoring 40 against Gonzaga and 30-plus in each of his first four NCAA tournament games. He set the NCAA single-season three-point record (162). As a junior he averaged 28.6 points, won the NCAA scoring title, became Davidson's all-time leading scorer, and was a consensus first-team All-American. He left after three seasons, later finishing a sociology degree in 2022. Davidson retired his No. 30.

## Golden State Warriors (2009–present)

On June 25, 2009 — Dell's 45th birthday — Golden State drafted Curry seventh. He averaged 17.5 points as a rookie, made All-Rookie First Team, and finished second in Rookie of the Year voting. Ankle injuries limited him in 2011–12. The Warriors included him in a proposed Andrew Bogut trade that Milwaukee rejected; Monta Ellis was traded instead, opening the starting job.

He signed a four-year, $44 million extension before 2012–13. With Klay Thompson he became half of the Splash Brothers. That season he set an NBA record with 272 threes and scored 54 with 11 threes at Madison Square Garden. In 2014–15 Steve Kerr became coach, Curry won MVP, and Golden State won its first title since 1975.

In 2015–16 he became the first unanimous MVP, the first player to average 30-plus while shooting 50–40–90, and led the league in scoring (30.1), steals, and free-throw percentage. He made a record 402 threes. The Warriors went 73–9, then blew a 3–1 Finals lead to Cleveland.

Golden State won titles in 2017 and 2018 after adding Kevin Durant. Curry signed a five-year, $201 million supermax in 2017. In 2019 the Warriors lost the Finals to Toronto; Curry scored a then playoff-high 47 in Game 3. A broken hand limited him to five games in 2019–20. In 2020–21 he scored a career-high 62 against Portland, passed Wilt Chamberlain as the Warriors' all-time scorer, and won a second scoring title at 32.0 points.

On December 14, 2021, at Madison Square Garden, he passed Ray Allen as the NBA's all-time leader in three-pointers made. In 2022 he won the inaugural Western Conference finals MVP, then Finals MVP as Golden State beat Boston for his fourth title. In 2023 he scored 50 in a Game 7 against Sacramento, the first 50-point Game 7 in NBA history. In 2023–24 he scored 60 at age 35 and won Clutch Player of the Year. In 2025 he became the first player to 4,000 career threes and won a second All-Star Game MVP. In 2025–26 Golden State missed the playoffs after injuries; Curry still posted consecutive 45-point games at age 37.

## Championships and awards

He has four NBA titles with Golden State: 2015, 2017, 2018, and 2022. He won NBA MVP in 2015 and was the first unanimous MVP in 2016. He was NBA Finals MVP in 2022, All-Star Game MVP in 2022 and 2025, and Clutch Player of the Year in 2024. He is a 12-time All-Star and an 11-time All-NBA selection (four First Team). He has two scoring titles (2016, 2021), two Three-Point Contest wins (2015, 2021), and led the league in threes a record eight times. He holds the highest career free-throw percentage in NBA history (.912). He is a member of the NBA 75th Anniversary Team.

## Playing style

Curry changed NBA spacing by making the deep three a primary weapon. He can release in under half a second with a high arc, and he shoots from 30-plus feet at a rate most players cannot match. Off-ball movement, screens, and pull-up threes force defenses to chase him, which opens layups and dump-offs for teammates like Draymond Green and Klay Thompson. That gravity is the core of Golden State's pace-and-space offense. He won Clutch Player of the Year after leading the league in clutch points, made field goals, and made threes in 2023–24.

## Olympics and Team USA

Curry won silver at the 2007 FIBA U-19 World Championship. He won FIBA World Cup gold in 2010 and 2014. He withdrew from the 2016 Olympics because of ankle and knee issues. His Olympic debut came in Paris 2024. In the semifinal against Serbia he scored 36 with nine threes in a 17-point comeback. In the gold-medal game against France he scored 24 and hit four straight late threes, including the "Golden Dagger." He led Team USA in scoring (14.8) and made the Olympics All-Star Five.

## Off the court

Curry married Ayesha Alexander in 2011. They have two daughters and two sons. His brother Seth is also an NBA player. He has keratoconus and wears contact lenses. His signature line with Under Armour (the Curry Brand) made him a face of the company for more than a decade. He funds golf at Howard University and won the American Century Championship celebrity golf event in 2023. He has donated mosquito nets through Nothing But Nets and helped launch President Obama's malaria initiative.

## Career statistics

Through 2025–26, Curry's regular-season career line is 1,069 games, 34.0 minutes, 24.8 points, 4.7 rebounds, 6.3 assists, and 1.5 steals, on .471 / .422 / .912 shooting. The free-throw rate is an NBA record. Playoff averages: 26.8 points, 5.3 rebounds, and 6.1 assists in 155 games. College at Davidson: 25.3 points, 4.5 rebounds, 3.7 assists in 104 games.

Season highs include 32.0 points (2020–21 scoring title), 30.1 points with 402 threes (2015–16), 8.5 assists (2013–14), and a 62-point game against Portland. Playoff highs include 30.5 points per game in 2023 and 50 points in the 2023 Game 7 against Sacramento.
